Our Story

How a family recipe box became a restaurant — and why most of it is still unwritten.

Three generations, one recipe box

Recipe Secrets started as exactly that: a battered tin box of handwritten recipe cards, kept by our founder's mother and passed down with strict instructions never to photocopy them. When we opened our doors, we kept the rule.

What's changed is who gets to taste the results. Dishes that once only appeared at family gatherings — the short rib, the Sunday pasta, the tart nobody will explain — are now on the menu every night, cooked exactly the way they always were.

We're not a fusion kitchen and we're not chasing trends. We're a family table that happens to seat 80.

Meet the Chef

Chef Lena Moreau

Head Chef & Keeper of the Recipe Box

Lena grew up in the kitchen the recipe box came from — first as the kid handed a wooden spoon to "stay busy," later as the only grandchild willing to learn every recipe by hand instead of asking for the card.

She's spent twelve years cooking professionally, but says the hardest dish she ever learned was still the first one: her grandmother's short rib, which took four attempts and one minor kitchen argument to get right.

The "Secret Recipes" Philosophy

We get asked for the recipes constantly. Here's why we still say no.

A recipe written down becomes an instruction. A recipe kept in someone's hands and taught by feel stays alive — adjusted by the cook, the season, the tomatoes that came in that morning. That's the version we want to serve you, not a laminated copy of it.

So we won't print the method. We will, happily, tell you the story behind the dish, pour you something good while you wait, and let the kitchen do the explaining.
